This weekend I was at Since AI in Turku, a Major League Hacking hackathon: pick a company's challenge, and build a working product in three days.

I chose Elisa's. Elisa runs a large part of Finland's telecom network: the calls, the data, thousands of units across the whole country.

Running a network like that is becoming more and more autonomous. Machines watch it, and machines fix it.

But the know-how of what actually works lives in people. It has built up over decades and it keeps building: in chats, in emails, in someone's head. Nobody writes it down, so the machines can't see it.

So I built Silent context: an agent that reads those chats as they are and pins each piece of advice to the right unit, with who said it and when. No forms, no extra people. The knowledge is captured as the work gets done.

Next time the same fault appears anywhere in the network, the autonomous system has the answer in one call: what to do, and why.

Three days, one agent, the code is public.
